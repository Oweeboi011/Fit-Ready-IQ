# UX Audit — Fit-Ready-IQ (2026-08-14)

A senior-UX review of the shipped product against its own stated strategy, covering feature gaps, enhancement opportunities and optimizations. Every claim below is anchored to a file path or an accepted ADR and was verified against the tree at the time of writing; nothing here is aspirational unless labelled as a recommendation.

Per ADR-0005, it cross-references `docs/adr/` and `docs/wiki/` rather than restating them.

> **Status (2026-08-14, same day):** the audit was written first and acted on second. Tiers 0, 2 (partly), 3 (partly) and 5 have since been implemented — see **§ Fixed in this change** at the end. Tier 1 (Trip and the safety timer) and Tier 4 (billing) remain open, and the reasons are recorded there rather than quietly dropped. Findings below are left as originally written so the sequencing argument still reads, with fixes marked inline.

---

## The structural finding

**The product answers "can I finish this route?" and then abandons the user.**

There is no way to say *I'm going*, no record that you went, and no payoff for having gone. Discovery, filtering, readiness, training plan, weather and GPX export all serve the moments *before* a trip. Nothing serves the trip itself or anything after it. The session ends where the commitment should begin.

This matters more than any individual missing feature, because four separate things are all blocked behind the same gap:

- **Retention** — nothing brings a user back; every visit starts from an empty map.
- **Safety** — the entire proposition in ADR-0003 lives here.
- **Emotional payoff** — an explorer's motivation is accumulation, and nothing accumulates.
- **Monetization** — a tool people don't return to is hard to charge for.

The project has already diagnosed this. **ADR-0003 ("Trip is the spine, and safety is a server-side timer") is accepted**, and `CONTEXT.md` fixes the vocabulary for it. But it has zero code, no `trips` collection, and — the finding that prompted this audit — **zero issues in the backlog**. The open backlog (#20–#34) covers the GPX route builder and agentic chat only. The most differentiating decision the project has made is not represented in the work queue at all.

---

## What is already strong (protect these)

Several things here are better than the shipped competition and should not be diluted by anything below.

- **`src/lib/readiness.ts`** — readiness gated by the **limiting factor (the MIN of the factors), not an average**, with the limiter named so the answer is actionable. "Your longest recent outing is 8 km against this route's 20 km" is a genuinely better product than a number. Averaging would hide exactly the thing that turns people back.
- **`src/lib/trainingPlan.ts`** — converts the readiness gap into a date and weekly targets at a conservative 10%/week, and explicitly disclaims being coaching.
- **A real `unknown` state.** `score: null` rather than a guessed zero, applied consistently across elevation, difficulty, advisories and weather. `/api/advisories` returning `configured: false` instead of "all clear" is the correct call on a safety-adjacent surface.
- **`src/lib/ui.ts`** — one primary action per viewport, enforced as a vocabulary rather than a guideline.
- **Honest self-documentation** — `roadmap.ts` and `docs/wiki/DEAD-CODE-AUDIT.md` name what is missing instead of implying completeness.

The recommendations below are written to extend this posture, not to trade it for growth features.

## Tier 0 — Truth debt

The codebase's first rule is that nothing is invented. **The marketing surface currently breaks that rule**, and it does so in front of the exact audience equipped to notice.

| Claim | Reality |
|---|---|
| `src/app/page.tsx:33` — "drop in a GPX, **TCX** or Apple Health export" | TCX is not accepted. `ConnectDevicesModal.tsx:24-31` sets `ACCEPTED_TRACK_FILES = '.gpx'` with the rationale: *"`parseGpxFile` reads `<trkpt>`, which TCX does not use… Advertise what we can actually read."* The upload component made the right call; the landing page contradicts it. |
| `src/app/page.tsx` — "Strava, Garmin, COROS, Komoot or Apple Health… about twenty seconds" | Only Strava is a real OAuth connect (`/api/strava/*`). The others are manual file-export instructions rendered per-device in `ConnectDevicesModal.tsx` — not twenty-second connects. |
| `src/lib/plans.ts` Guide tier — "25 athletes", "group trip planning and shared itineraries", "priority support" | None exist. There is no multi-user, group, or sharing primitive anywhere in the product. |
| Landing hero, labelled "real UI, not a mockup image" | Hardcoded Mount Pulag / readiness 82 placeholder markup. The numbers are invented. |

Two internal docs are stale in the same direction and should be corrected alongside: `src/lib/gpxParser.ts:2` claims "(and basic TCX)", and `.claude/CLAUDE.md`'s activity-sources table lists "GPX/TCX file upload" for COROS/Garmin/Komoot.

**Recommendation: fix the copy to match what ships — do not build the features to match the copy.** This is hours of work and it is the cheapest credibility win available.

## Tier 1 — Close the loop: Trip and safety (ADR-0003)

Nothing exists: no Trip entity, no `trips` collection in `firestore.rules`, no check-in, no emergency contact, no scheduled function. `docs/wiki/USER-FLOW.md:150` states it plainly — "no onboarding, gear-list, or check-in flow exists yet."

**The hidden prerequisite nobody has scoped:** there is no notification channel of any kind in the repo — no FCM, no email provider, no scheduled function. A dead-man's switch that cannot notify anyone is not a safety feature. ADR-0003 does not call this out, and it should be the first issue filed.

Recommended shape:

- **Make onboarding goal capture the seed of the first Trip.** Today onboarding is a single dismissible toast (`src/components/map/MapNotices.tsx:27-51`), whose own comment concedes *"There is no onboarding anywhere in the product: a first-time visitor on a phone sees a spinning map, a hamburger and nothing else."* Asking *"what are you training for?"* on first run sets a target Place/Route, gives readiness something to aim at, and produces a Trip almost for free. It is the highest-leverage onboarding change available and it directly serves ADR-0003.
- **Trip** = target + date + expected return + emergency contact + gear + readiness snapshot + weather window.
- **Server-side dead-man's switch**, per ADR-0003 — the traveller's device is deliberately uninvolved, which is precisely why it works in a canyon with no signal.
- **Check-in closes the Trip and prompts to attach the matching Activity.** Safety and history are the same feature; this is the design's best idea and it should not be split into two projects.
- **A read-only link for the emergency contact** ("they're due back at 18:00"). Per `CONTEXT.md`, the contact is explicitly *not* a user of the product — no account, no app.

Renaming the dormant `itineraries` collection (present in `firestore.rules`) and the backend `Itinerary` entity to Trip is nearly free today and expensive once either goes live.

## Tier 2 — Explorer emotion: progression and the record

Everything in this tier is computable **honestly from Activity data already imported**. No new external data, no new integrations, no invention. This is the cheapest large win in the audit, and it is what serves a user motivated by thrill and exploration.

### Invert readiness from a filter into a recommender

Today the user must find a route, open it, and *then* learn whether they're ready. `computeReadiness()` already runs client-side across the loaded candidate set — sorting by it inverts the entire product:

- **"Routes you're ready for this weekend"** — readiness ≥ 80.
- **"Your next step up"** — the **stretch band, readiness 60–80**: hard enough to matter, achievable enough to attempt. This is the progression ladder ADR-0003 refers to.

This needs no new infrastructure and no new data. It is the single highest value-to-effort item in this document, and it converts readiness from a verdict into a reason to open the app.

### The record

- **Frontier map (fog-of-war).** Render imported activity polylines as territory actually travelled. It turns the map from a search tool into a personal record, and it is pure rendering of data already in hand. For an exploration-motivated user this is the strongest single mechanic available.
- **Vertical ledger** — year-to-date and lifetime metres climbed. `src/lib/fitnessScore.ts` is deliberately month-to-date and resets with the month. `ProfileModal` does already show lifetime distance, elevation and moving time, so this is narrower than "nothing accumulates" — what is missing is the year-to-date frame, personal records, and any sense of scale for a number like 78,000 m. (Its inline `reduce` calls are also unguarded: one GPX with no elevation track yields `NaN` and every lifetime figure renders as "NaN".)
- **Peaks bagged** — match Activity geometry against Place summits. Per ADR-0003 the matcher may *suggest* but must never write history unasked: a false "you climbed this" destroys trust in every readiness judgement downstream.
- **Honest personal records** — longest day, biggest climb, and comparison against *your own* prior efforts on a repeat.

> **Note for reviewers:** the personal-records item is **not** a revival of the Strava-segment feature ADR-0004 removed. That was rejected because segments were generated from the place name — fabricated data. Comparing a user's own recorded Activities against each other uses real data they produced, and is the legitimate version of the same instinct.

## Tier 3 — Field-readiness: PWA, offline, mobile

For an app used on a mountain, this is the most glaring practical gap. `src/frontend/public/` contains only `.gitkeep` and `icon.svg`: **no web app manifest, no service worker, no `viewport` export in `layout.tsx`, not installable.** The only offline handling anywhere is a `navigator.onLine` check producing a sign-in error string (`src/lib/firebaseAuthErrors.ts:21-22`).

- **Web app manifest + icons + `display: standalone`** — hours of work, unblocks installability, and is the difference between a website and something a user takes up a hill.
- **Offline Trip pack**, per ADR-0003: route line, elevation profile, gear, contacts, queued check-in. **Map tiles stay explicitly out of scope** — Google Maps' terms forbid caching them, and doing so would mean migrating off Google Maps entirely. That remains a separate bet, correctly deferred.
- **Sunlight legibility and one-handed reach.** The dock and modals are designed for a desk. High-contrast outdoor mode and thumb-reachable primary controls are real ergonomics for the actual usage context, and are currently unconsidered.

Responsive layout does exist (Tailwind breakpoints, mobile hamburger sidebar, a mobile-specific first-run string) — but that is layout, not a mobile posture.

## Tier 4 — Make Pro real

**The paywall is entirely cosmetic.** `hasEntitlement()` in `src/lib/plans.ts:146` is exported and unit-tested but **is never called anywhere in the application** — the only references outside the module are in `plans.test.ts`. There is no `currentPlan` concept, no stored trial start date, no expiry check, no billing dependency, no checkout route, and no `subscriptions` collection. Signing in grants Strava sync, readiness and the AI coach free, permanently. The only runtime effect of choosing a tier is `rememberSelectedPlan()` writing to `localStorage`.

`plans.ts:105` says so itself: *"Where the plan a visitor clicked is remembered until billing exists."*

**The recommendation is deliberately not "add Stripe now":**

- Pro is sold on readiness, and per ADR-0004 readiness is still scored against **fabricated route metrics** — Places-pin distances where `distance_km` is the *driving distance from the user to the trailhead*, not the length of any path. Charging for readiness before ADR-0004 lands monetizes the exact credibility problem that ADR was written to fix.
- Charging before the loop is closed means charging for a tool people do not return to.

Correct order: fix the claims (Tier 0) → land real geometry (ADR-0004) → close the loop (Tier 1) → then wire entitlements and billing.

## Tier 5 — Optimizations and defects

### Bundle and rendering

- **The heavy modals are all statically imported.** `src/app/app/page.tsx:11-22` pulls in `DetailsModal` (1,844 lines — the largest file in the repo), `ConnectDevicesModal`, `ProfileModal`, `ChatBot`, `RoadmapModal` and `AdminModal` at module scope, so all of them ship in the initial `/app` bundle for **every visitor** — including the admin console to users who can never open it. The only dynamic import in the app is `MapView` (`src/components/map/MapCanvas.tsx:13`), and that is for SSR avoidance rather than bundle size. Converting the six modals to `next/dynamic` is the highest value-to-risk optimization available.
- **`src/app/app/page.tsx`** — 818 lines, 35 React state/effect hook calls in a single `Home` component. State this centralized means a filter keystroke re-renders the whole shell, map area included.
- `/app` is fully client-rendered; first paint waits on JS plus the Google Maps loader.
- `next/image` migration and the WCAG 2.2 AA audit remain open per `roadmap.ts`.

### Security defect (Firestore rules)

`firestore.rules` gates `training_sessions` on authentication only, with **no ownership check**:

```
match /training_sessions/{sessionId} {
  allow read, write: if isAuthenticated();
}
```

Every sibling collection (`activities`, `training_programs`, `itineraries`) correctly checks `request.auth.uid == resource.data.user_id`. As written, any signed-in user can read or write any other user's training sessions. The collection is not yet populated by the frontend, which is why this has not surfaced — but it should be fixed before anything writes to it, not after.

### The missing weather answer

ADR-0004 states that current conditions "cannot answer the only weather question a planner has: *when should I go?*" — yet no weather-window computation exists (`grep` for `weatherWindow`/`bestWindow` returns nothing). `src/lib/weatherAlerts.ts` already classifies forecast hours into hazards, so deriving the best contiguous window for a specific Route is a short step from what ships and closes a stated architectural intent. `CONTEXT.md` already defines "weather window" as a property of a Route-and-forecast pairing.

### Keep persona branching deferred

Four personas are documented in `SOLUTION-PLAN.md` §3 with a full feature matrix, and no UI branches on any of them. This is the right call for now, and the audit recommends keeping it: SOLUTION-PLAN already blocks persona work on ADR-0004, and persona is best expressed as a *lens over Trip and readiness* rather than as a standalone feature. Building it before the spine exists would mean building four variations of a product that still forgets you.

### Deliberately not done

- **Tier 1 — Trip and the safety timer.** Not started. It needs a notification channel that does not exist, a Firestore schema, and a scheduled function; and per this audit's own sequencing it should follow ADR-0004's real route geometry, since a Trip built on fabricated route metrics inherits the credibility problem. Starting it half-way would leave a `trips` collection with no way to raise an alert — worse than not having one, because it would look like a safety net.
- **Tier 4 — billing.** Not started, deliberately, for the reason argued above: readiness is still scored against fabricated route metrics, so gating it behind Pro monetizes the problem ADR-0004 exists to fix. It also needs a payment provider account and live credentials, which are the owner's to create. `hasEntitlement()` is still uncalled.
- **ADR-0004 route geometry.** Unchanged. It is the largest item in the audit and a genuine multi-week piece of work — three producers, a new routing provider, and a data-model migration.
- **Offline Trip pack.** Blocked on Trip existing.

## Open questions for the owner

1. **Is Guide (multi-athlete) a real near-term tier, or aspirational?** It currently promises three features with no backing code and no multi-user primitive anywhere. If aspirational, the copy should say "coming" or the tier should be withdrawn until it exists.
2. **Does the free tier keep readiness?** Pro is sold on it, but it is what makes the product worth returning to. Gating it may starve the loop this audit recommends building.
3. **Peaks-bagged matching threshold** — ADR-0003 requires human confirmation, but how close a track must pass to a summit before it is *suggested* is a product decision, not a technical one.
